Draconic Crest

A wave whose crest arrives parallel to the beach breaks along its whole length at once. An oblique crest breaks point by point, so the beach never takes the full blow. The gold ring is the equatorial stratosphere where the QBO lives. On the left, the tropical (declination) front is tied to the Moon's overhead point and cuts the ring at two spots that run around it. On the right, the draconic front is the Moon's height above the ecliptic. It does not depend on longitude, so it meets every point of the ring at the same instant. The Sun and the seasons are shown in both views: the summer hemisphere greens, the winter one snows. The gate opens at the equinoxes, when the Sun's ray lies in the plane of the ring. The white circle is the Moon's real orbit. Hold the ecliptic level turns the view so the Sun's path is flat: Earth, its ring and the fronts then show their 23.4° tilt, and the Moon's orbit lies within 5.1° of the Sun's path.

What the equinox gate keeps

Twice a year the Sun crosses the equator and the gate opens. Each bar is the front's signed value at that moment. The parallel front builds a clean 2.37-year oscillation, the period the QBO actually has. The oblique front's samples make a 2.72-year pattern that the QBO does not show.

Why the textbook strength is too low

Tidal tables measure the force at the shore: the instantaneous tidal potential at a point. In that potential a pure 27.212-day line is exactly zero, and in the next-order term it is about 1/60 of the main terms, so the draconic force looks negligible.

Measured along the crest, the draconic swing is sin 5.145° = 0.090, the same size as the Mf term (0.079) in the same units. Because it arrives at every longitude at once, the gate captures all of it in one blow instead of a running series of small ones. That is the physical assumption of the model: the ring responds to the Moon's latitude from the Sun's plane, gated at the equinoxes. The 2.37-year period the QBO picks is the evidence for it.
